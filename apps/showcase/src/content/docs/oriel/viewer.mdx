# Viewer controls

The viewer opens images and videos over the gallery.

![Oriel's viewer showing an image, with its controls along the top](/screenshots/oriel/view-viewer.png)

## Opening and closing

- Double-click a grid item or press Enter / F.
- Press Escape to close and return to the grid.
- The top bar names the file and shows its size and format (and, for videos, length, dimensions, and codec), with **Copy path**, **Reveal in folder** (shows the file in Finder), **Show in Tidy** (opens the file in Tidy, leaving View as it is), **Fullscreen**, and **Close**.

The controls and the pointer hide after a couple of seconds without movement. A paused video keeps them in sight.

## Image navigation

- **Q** or **Arrow Left**: previous item
- **E** or **Arrow Right**: next item
- On-screen arrows at the sides do the same.

**Loop viewer navigation** (on by default) wraps previous/next at the ends of the list. With it off, the arrows disable at the ends. Sort order and recursive settings still apply to what is in the list.

## Video playback

| Control | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Play / pause | Space or 2 (or click the video) |
| Seek backward 5s | 1 |
| Seek forward 5s | 3 |
| Temporary speed boost (2×) | Hold 4 |
| Mute / unmute | M |

The on-screen bar also has a seek bar that shows the time under the pointer, a volume panel with a slider and **Mute**, and a **Playback speed** panel from 0.5× to 2×. Volume and mute are remembered between launches; speed lasts while the viewer stays open. **Autoplay videos in viewer** and **Loop videos** in Settings apply here.

Video plays through the bundled libmpv, so MKV and WebM play as well as MP4 and MOV, and AV1 seeks exactly.

## Fullscreen

Use the **Fullscreen** button in the viewer's top bar or `Ctrl+Cmd+F` (F11 off macOS). Both make the whole window full screen.

## GIFs and large images

GIFs animate. Still images scale to fit the viewport; there is no zoom. Very large files may take a moment to decode, so wait before skipping to the next item if the screen is blank. A file that cannot be decoded says so in place of the image, and Tidy's Library lists it under **Won't open**.
